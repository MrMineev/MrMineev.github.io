Degree of a \(p\)-sheeted Covering Map (Exercise)

Algebraic Topology · 🗓️ November 2, 2026

Problem 1  (Hatcher Exercise 3.3.9)

Show that a \(p\) sheeted covering space projection \(M \rightarrow N\) has degree \(\pm p\), when \(M\) and \(N\) are connected closed orientable manifolds.

Proof. First let us prove the following relatively simple fact,

Proposition 1.1  (Hatcher Exercise 3.3.8)

For a map \(f : M \rightarrow N\) between connected closed orientable \(n\) manifolds, suppose there is a ball \(B \subset N\) such that \(f^{-1}(B)\) is the disjoint union of balls \(B_i\) each mapped homeomorphically by \(f\) onto \(B\). Show the degree of \(f\) is \(\sum \epsilon_i\) where \(\epsilon_i\) is \(\pm 1\) according to whether \(f : B_i \rightarrow B\) preserves or reverses local orientations induced from given fundamental classes \([M]\) and \([N]\).

Let \(x \in B\), then \(\deg f = \sum_{i = 1}^{k} \deg f_{x_i}\), where \(\{x_1, \ldots, x_k\} = f^{-1}(x)\). Observe that the local degree is given by the degree of the map \(H_n(M|x_i) \rightarrow H_n(N|x)\), however by Excision this is the degree of the induced map, \[ H_n(B_i | x_i) \overset{g}{\longrightarrow} H_n(B|x) \] and since \(f : B_i \rightarrow B\) is a homeomorphism, it induces an isomorphism on the homology groups, thus \(g\) is an isomorphism and thus \(\deg g= \pm 1\), which is defined as \(\epsilon_i\).

Let \(x \in N\). Since \(M\) and \(N\) are both closed orientable manifolds, we only need to show that \(\epsilon_i\) are all equal (because then the sum is \(\pm p\)), since \(M\) may be covered by \(p\) sheets of \(N\). The trick is to define the following two sets, \[ \begin{cases} M_+ = \{y \in M \mid f_{\star}(\mu_y) = \mu_{f(y)}\} \\ M_- = \{y \in M \mid f_{\star}(\mu_y) = -\mu_{f(y)}\} \end{cases} \] and show that they are open.

Let \(x \in N\) and let \(x_1, \dots, x_p\) be its preimages in \(M\). Choose a small ball \(B\) around \(x\) such that \(f^{-1}(B) = \bigcup B_i\), where each \(B_i\) is an open ball containing \(x_i\) and \(f : B_i \to B\) is a homeomorphism. To show \(M_+\) is open, let \(x_i \in M_+\). We claim that the open ball \(B_i\) is entirely contained in \(M_+\). For any \(y_1 \in B_i\), let \(y = f(y_1) \in B\). The local behavior of the map \(f\) around \(y_1\) is identical to its behavior around \(x_i\) because \(f\) is a homeomorphism on \(B_i\). Thus, \(f_*(\mu_{y_1}) = \mu_y\), showing that \(B_i \subseteq M_+\).

consequently, since every point in \(M_+\) has an open neighborhood (since we can repeat the argument for a point in the open neighborhood of the image under \(f\)), it must be that both \(M_+\) and \(M_-\) are open!

Thus, \(M_+\) and \(M_-\) form a disjoint open cover of \(M\), which implies that the space is disconnected, contradiction, thus one of the spaces it empty and the other is full! Thus all the points in \(M\) have the same local orientation and thus we are done!
