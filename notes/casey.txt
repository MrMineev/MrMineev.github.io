Inverting in $A$ we can rephrase the problem as given a $\triangle{DEF}$, let $A$ be the $F$-extouch point and let $C$ be the intersection of the line through $A$ parallel to $DF$ with $EF$. Then we want to show that the circle passing through $A$ and $C$ tangent to $DE$ must be tangent to the $D$-excircle.

Let $P$ be the $D$-extouch point. It feels like we want to apply Casey's theorem, however to make it work we need to identity a third point, it must be one of the intersections with the sides, thus the only natural choice is $Y = \Gamma \cap EF$. Observe that $\angle{AYC} = \angle{DAC} = 180^{\circ} - \angle{EDF}$, thus it must be that $DAYF$ is cyclic!

Now, we are ready to apply Casey's theorem to $AYC$, and consequently we must show that,
\[
    AC \times YP - YC \times AQ + AY \times CP = 0
  \]observe that $AQ = DF$ (since $DA = PF$), then we may rewrite the equation as, (where $p$ is the half perimeter of $\triangle{DEF}$)

\begin{align*}
    \frac{DF \times AE}{DE} \times \left( EP - EC \right) - \left( EC - EY \right) \times DF + \frac{DF \times AE}{EF} \times \left( EP - CP \right)
    \\ = \frac{DF(p - EF)}{DE} \left( (p - DE) - \frac{DE \cdot (p - EF)}{EF} \right) - \left( \frac{EF \times AE}{DE} - \frac{DE \times AE}{EF} \right) \times DF
    \\ + \frac{DF \times AE}{EF} \left( (p - DE) - \frac{EF \times AE}{DE} \right)
  \end{align*}now let us rewrite $a, b, c = EF, DF, DE$ then we want to show that,
\begin{align*}
    &\frac{b(b + c - a)}{2c}\left( \frac{a + b - c}{2} - \frac{c(b + c - a)}{2a} \right) - \left( \frac{a(b + c - a)}{2c} - \frac{c(b + c - a)}{2a} \right) b \\
    &+ \frac{b(b + c - a)}{2a} \left( \frac{a + b - c}{2} - \frac{a(b + c - a)}{2c} \right) \\
    &\cong \frac{1}{2c}\left( \frac{a + b - c}{2} - \frac{c(b + c - a)}{2a} \right) - \left( \frac{a}{2c} - \frac{c}{2a} \right) + \frac{1}{2a}\left( \frac{a + b - c}{2} - \frac{a(b + c - a)}{2c} \right) \\
    &= \frac{a + b - c}{4c} - \frac{b + c - a}{4a} - \frac{a}{2c} + \frac{c}{2a} + \frac{a + b - c}{4a} - \frac{b + c - a}{4c} \\
    &= \left( \frac{a + b - c}{4c} - \frac{2a}{4c} - \frac{b + c - a}{4c} \right) + \left( -\frac{b + c - a}{4a} + \frac{2c}{4a} + \frac{a + b - c}{4a} \right) \\
    &= \left( \frac{a + b - c - 2a - b - c + a}{4c} \right) + \left( \frac{-b - c + a + 2c + a + b - c}{4a} \right) \\
    &= -\frac{2c}{4c} + \frac{2a}{4a} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 0
  \end{align*}where by $\cong$ I meant dividing by $b(b + c - a)$, and thus we are done! $\blacksquare$

Circles $\omega$ and $\Omega$ meet at points $A$ and $B$. Let $M$ be the midpoint of the arc $AB$ of circle $\omega$ ($M$ lies inside $\Omega$). A chord $MP$ of circle $\omega$ intersects $\Omega$ at $Q$ ($Q$ lies inside $\omega$). Let $\ell_P$ be the tangent line to $\omega$ at $P$, and let $\ell_Q$ be the tangent line to $\Omega$ at $Q$. Prove that the circumcircle of the triangle formed by the lines $\ell_P$, $\ell_Q$ and $AB$ is tangent to $\Omega$.

\begin{proof}
  Let $X = \ell_P \cap AB$, $Y = \ell_Q \cap AB$ and $Z = \ell_P \cap \ell_Q$, then by Casey's theorem it is enough to show that,
  \[
    ZQ \times XY + YQ \times XZ = XP \times YZ
  \]
  however since $XP = XR$ (since if we intersect $S = \ell_P \cap \ell_M$ then clearly $SP = SM$, then since $XR \parallel SM$ by homothety we obtain the equality) thus $XP \times YZ = XR \times YZ$! By Menelaus on $\triangle{XYZ}$ we obtain,
  \[
    \frac{ZP}{XP} \cdot \frac{XR}{YR} \cdot \frac{YQ}{ZQ} = 1
  \]
  thus $\frac{ZP}{YR} \cdot \frac{YQ}{ZQ} = \frac{XZ + XP}{XP - XY} \cdot \frac{YQ}{ZQ} = 1$ thus $XZ \cdot YQ + XP \cdot YQ = ZQ \cdot XP - XY \cdot ZQ$ thus $XZ \cdot YQ + XY \cdot ZQ = XP \cdot YZ$ which is what we wanted to show!
\end{proof}
